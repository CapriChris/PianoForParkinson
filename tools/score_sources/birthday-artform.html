How to learn Happy Birthday

  • The 4 chords that carry it (C6, G7, C and F), and where each one sits under the tune.
  • Hands apart first, then together at 60 bpm.
  • An early intermediate piece, under a minute long: short steps from the first listen to the whole thing.
  1. Hear it through at Slow (60 bpm). Open it in the reader, choose Slow, and follow the page with your eyes without playing.
  2. Learn the right hand of bars 1 to 8 on its own, in C major. Say the note names out loud the first time through, then play it three times without stopping.
  3. Find the chords under it: C6, G7, C and F. Play each one as a block in your left hand and say its name, so you know what the harmony is before the left hand has to move.
  4. Learn the left hand of the same bars on its own. Keep it quiet and even: it is the floor the tune stands on.
  5. Put hands together at Slow, 60 bpm. Bars 1 to 8 only. Stop after every wrong note and play that bar again before moving on.
  6. Move up to Steady (90 bpm) once Slow feels easy three times in a row. Then learn the next 8 bars the same way: right hand, left hand, together.
  7. Play the whole piece (under a minute) from the start, at whatever speed keeps it going. A steady run with a few slips beats a fast one that stops.

About this piece

Two schoolteachers wrote the tune in 1893; a court finally freed the words in 2016. The most sung music on earth, and the piece every pianist is ambushed with at parties.

How to play it

Learn it in this key, then try it in three others: the ambush never comes in the key you practised.

Reading it, and printing it

The score opens in Artform's reader, which shows the music as engraved notation and plays it back so you can hear the line before you try it. You can slow the tempo down, loop a passage that is not working yet, and print the page when you would rather have paper on the stand.

The file itself is MusicXML, which MuseScore, Sibelius, Finale and Dorico all open, so the notes are yours to keep whatever you read them in.
